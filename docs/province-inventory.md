# Province inventory

The catalog is a transcription of the AH1996 printed board in
[BGG photograph 192678](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/192678/age-of-renaissance),
checked against the area-values table (§6.2, PDF page 12) and commodity reference sheets
(PDF pages 18–21) in the
[errata, FAQ and player-aid bundle](https://www.goblins.net/files/downloads/Age%20of%20Renaissance.pdf).
Access requirements follow [rules §2](rules.md#2-components) and §6.4, including the
official May 1998 errata on the bundle's first page. Identifiers normalize names to lowercase
ASCII words separated by hyphens; they are catalog identities, independent of presentation labels.
For example, `strasbourg`, `nuremberg`, and `novgorod` normalize variant spellings in the aids.

The photograph contains 68 mainland commodity markets, 35 satellites and five overseas boxes:
108 provinces in total. A satellite's printed square is represented as market number 1 and
has no commodity. An overseas box belongs to no geographical area and counts toward each
of its printed commodities. Access columns record required advances, separately from shipping
capacity, movement distance, and the overseas occupancy limit.

The board resolves the previously open geographical questions: there is no named Loire
market; Area I has six satellites, including the narrow Smolensk province extending toward
Kiev; Libya is in Area VI, inside its green boundary. Bergen and Kongsberg belong to Area II.

The FAQ area-values table has arithmetic/transcription errors: its market rows sum to 73
including overseas boxes, while its printed total and six-player row say 71. Its three-player
market total says 46, while its area rows and the board sum to 48. Its Area VII token total
says 8, while the thirteen printed market numbers sum to 48. The catalog follows the
individual board provinces, rather than these inconsistent summary totals. Commodity aid
spellings vary between sheets; the photographed area outlines control geographical membership.

“Coastal” records contact with printed coastal water. Rivers and thick area boundaries do
not independently create coastal access. Dijon is inland, as explicitly stated in the
rulebook's §4.1. The capitals are the six identically named markets.
